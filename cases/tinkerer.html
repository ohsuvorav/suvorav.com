AI runs my life: three agents I built for myself

TL;DR

Orbit plans my days, Midas watches the family money, Mirality writes my LinkedIn posts. Each one learns from my corrections, so I explain things once, not every time.

Context

I'm a heavy AI user. I know where the tools shine and where they break, because I hit the edges every day.

At work I built AI-first ops for a team. At home I kept doing the same thing for myself: every time something annoyed me twice, I turned it into an agent.

Problem

Out-of-the-box assistants don't compound. I tried Claude and ChatGPT Projects, built-in memory, always-on bots. Three things kept failing:

  • Context doesn't self-update. I kept re-explaining the same facts about my life.
  • Built-in memory goes stale, and I can't see or fix what it "remembers".
  • Always-on requires my laptop to be online, or a hosted bot I can't control.

So the bar for anything I build is simple: the less context I have to repeat, the better it works.

Approach

Everything starts from one place: a "second brain" in Git. Principles per life area, a timeline of decisions, goals and projects in Notion. Every agent reads from it, so none of them starts from zero.

Orbit — a 24/7 chief of staff

Orbit is my personal assistant in Telegram. The name comes from its role: the central point of contact, like a chief of staff for all my agents and projects.

Imagine the following:

  • I send a voice message on the way home: "dinner with Sasha on Tuesday, and I still need to pay the accountant".
  • Orbit transcribes it, pulls my calendar, open tasks and principles from the second brain.
  • It creates the task in Notion, then reads it back to prove it's actually there.
  • In the morning it briefs me on email and what's in focus today.

The read-back part matters most. An early version happily replied "done, saved!" while nothing was written. I audited a few days of chats: a dozen tasks claimed, zero in Notion. Since then the rule is strict: no "done" without an external result.

Every mistake like that becomes an eval case: what failed, the criterion, the fix, and a check that a rephrased version of the same request passes too.

It runs on OpenClaw as the agent framework, so I don't maintain infra myself, and it's model-agnostic: I can switch to the best or cheapest model at any time.

Midas — the family finance partner

Midas started as a spending tracker and grew into a financial partner for my family.

  • The app syncs our bank accounts, categorizes transactions and splits them into family and personal budgets.
  • The agent lives in Telegram. I ask "how much did we spend on food in September?" or "how long is our runway?" and it answers from the real ledger, not a guess.

Money is where I trust AI the least, so Midas has the tightest boundaries of all three: read-only access to the ledger, exact calculations in code instead of LLM math, and nothing that moves money. When it wants to change its own code, it builds a patch in an isolated copy and waits for my review.

Mirality — a LinkedIn ghostwriter that keeps my voice

I'm not a native English speaker. My posts came out either too humble or too robotic. Generic AI writers made it worse: they sound like everyone else.

Mirality translates the voice, not the language. I dump a raw thought, it drafts a post using my brand strategy, hooks, checklist and best past posts as context.

The interesting part is under the hood. Every change to a post is an immutable version: AI draft, AI refine, or my own edit. My edits are the gold signal — the gap between what AI wrote and what I actually published is exactly what it needs to learn.

  • Context files are versioned like code, and each draft remembers which versions it used.
  • A file version is "better" if I edit less after it. That's the eval.
  • After publishing, post analytics flow back in, so it learns from what performed, not just from what I liked.

Started as a tool for me, it's now a multi-user app with an open-source client.

The pattern

Three different jobs, one recipe:

  • Shared context. Every agent reads the same second brain, so I never repeat myself.
  • Corrections are data. When I fix the output, the fix gets stored and reused next time.
  • Proof over promises. An action counts only when the result is visible outside the chat.
  • Boring infra. Telegram as UI, one cheap server, subscriptions instead of API bills.

I'd rather ship a scrappy agent I use tomorrow than a perfect one I demo once.
